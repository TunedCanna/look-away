Stepping away

Keep it running in the background
  1. Give it its own window. Drag this tab out of Edge so it sits in its own window, then minimise it. Or install it: in Edge select ··· → Apps → Install this site as an app. You can then pin it to the taskbar and have it start when you sign in.
  2. Stop Edge putting it to sleep. Edge Settings → System and performance → Never put these sites to sleep → Add, and paste this page's address. (The wording moves around a little between Edge versions.)
  3. Allow pop-ups. When Edge asks to show notifications, choose Allow. Missed it? Select the icon just left of the address, then Permissions for this site → Notifications → Allow. If Edge won't let you turn them on, your workplace has switched them off. That's fine: the chime and the tab title still tell you when it's time. Windows Do not disturb hides pop-ups too.
  4. Lock your screen when you leave (Windows + L). With "Pause while my screen is locked" ticked, it won't chime at an empty desk.

Other easy wins for tired eyes
  • Make text bigger instead of leaning in. Ctrl + + zooms Edge, Outlook and most Office apps. For everything at once: Windows Settings → Accessibility → Text size.
  • Match brightness to the room. A screen much brighter or darker than its surroundings is tiring. Try not to sit with a bright window right behind or in front of the screen.
  • Distance and height. Roughly an arm's length away, with the top of the screen at or just below eye level.
  • Blink on purpose. We blink far less while staring at a screen. A few slow, full blinks during each break helps with dry, gritty eyes.
  • Warmer light later in the day. Windows Settings → System → Display → Night light.
  • Get them checked. If your eyes keep feeling rough, book an eye test, and ask HR whether your workplace covers it for screen users. Many employers do, and in the UK it's a legal requirement.
